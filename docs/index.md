# Share Your Local AI: A Beginner Manual for One Household

Welcome! This is a friendly, step-by-step guide to running AI **on your own family's computers** — no cloud, no subscriptions, no data leaving your home.

## What this guide is

Maybe you've used a chatbot on the internet and thought, "It'd be nice if the whole family could use something like this — without everything going through some company's servers." Good news: you can.

The idea is simple. You put **Ollama**, a free program that runs AI models, on one computer in your house — the "main computer." Then, with a few careful settings, the other devices in your home (your laptop, a tablet, the phone in your pocket, a web browser on the couch) can use that same AI, over your home network, just like a shared family printer.

Everything stays inside your walls. Nothing gets uploaded. Your questions, your documents, your family's words — none of it goes anywhere you didn't put it.

## What this guide is *not*

To be upfront, this guide is **education only**:

- It is **not** medical advice, legal advice, or a security audit. (Dylan, who wrote this, has been a registered nurse, and one thing nurse training teaches you: know your scope. AI answering questions about your family's health is general information, not care.)
- It is **not** a homelab tinkering manual. If you want Docker, VLANs, reverse proxies and custom domains, a dedicated **homelab self-hosting guide** will serve you better.
- It is **not** "how do I install Ollama on my laptop so *I* can play with it." For getting started with local AI on a **single machine**, see a *local AI handbook*–style guide first; come back here when one computer isn't enough.

This guide lives in the exact gap between those two: **one household, one main computer, several friendly devices.**

## How to use it

Read pages 1–2 at the pace you'd like, follow 3–5 with both hands on the keyboard, and keep the checklist and troubleshooting pages open in another tab. You can finish in an afternoon. If you stop halfway, every page tells you how far along you are and what's safe to leave for later.
